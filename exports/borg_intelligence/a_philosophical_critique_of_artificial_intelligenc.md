## 🧠 Technical Summary
The article analyzes the 'magical thinking' associated with advanced AI through a philosophical lens, specifically challenging the notion that a sufficiently advanced simulation constitutes real intelligence. Feser deconstructs the operation of modern computing into logic gates (AND, OR, NOT) and demonstrates that these physical devices merely exploit a causal parallelism to logical functions. He argues that because the meaning, information, and logic within a computer are entirely dependent on human convention and intentionality—much like ink on paper or positions of valves—the 'intelligence' resides in the designer and user rather than the machine itself. The text posits that AI is an exercise in mimicry, fundamentally indistinguishable from a non-intelligent physical process when stripped of human interpretation.
